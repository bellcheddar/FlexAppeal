What the state score means

For each motif, s compares how far it sits from the nearest active reference against how far from the nearest inactive one, as (dinactive − dactive) / (dinactive + dactive). It runs from +1, sitting on the active references, to −1 on the inactive ones. A normalised difference rather than a raw gap, so a three-residue motif and a twenty-residue loop land on the same axis instead of the larger one dominating.

Both distances are measured after superposing on the structural core, so what is reported is the motif moving rather than the whole protein drifting. conflict means the geometry and the published rule-based criterion disagree, which is shown rather than resolved.

Why these sit beside the state score rather than inside it

These are the published criteria for the protein family: the αC salt bridge and the regulatory spine for a kinase, the TM3–TM6 cleft and the ionic lock for a receptor. They are measurements, and the state score is an interpretation, so they are kept apart. Where one contradicts the score the motif is labelled conflict rather than averaged.

Helix rotations and the connector RMSD are measured against the start model after superposing on the structural core, so what is reported is the helix moving rather than the whole protein drifting. A structure compared with itself reads exactly zero, which is asserted in the tests.

State score over time

How to read this

One line per motif, with the whole-protein score drawn in black on top: it is the residue-weighted mean of the motif scores, so a twenty-residue loop counts for more than a single tryptophan. Above +0.2 the geometry is calling a motif active-like and below −0.2 inactive-like; between them it is intermediate, and the bands are drawn to show that.

Where the run is long enough, the black line is a centred {{ "%.0f"|format(2) }} ns mean and the raw per-frame values are the faint line behind it. The smoothing is for reading the plot only: what is stored and what every number in the tables comes from is the raw series. On a run shorter than twice the window the smoothing is skipped rather than drawn, because a window wider than the data collapses every point to one value and that draws as a flat trace.

Why most of these say neither

Secondary structure is far less discriminating than geometry. Most residues of a transmembrane helix are helical in both states, so every agreement here is high and the differences are small. A lean is only called above a 0.05 margin, which was chosen from the observed spread: the motifs that discriminate separate by 0.07 to 0.15 and the ones carrying no signal by 0.02 to 0.04.

Where this does agree with the state score it is worth more than either alone, because the two share none of their machinery. For the kinase the hinge discriminates by neither measure: 0.18 Å from active against 0.16 from inactive, and secondary-structure agreements identical to three decimal places.

How to read the ribbon

One column per saved frame, one row per residue, coloured by the DSSP class. A motif unwinding shows up as a horizontal band changing colour part-way across. Computed with the full eight-class DSSP rather than the simplified three, because the difference between an α helix and a 3-10 helix is the kind of local change worth seeing.

A residue DSSP could not assign is left blank rather than drawn as coil: no assignment and an assignment of coil are different claims, and only one of them is evidence of anything.

{% if has_map %}

Reference state map

What this plane is

Every reference structure plotted by the geometry its state is read from, coloured and shaped by the catalogue's own label. For a kinase this is the Dunbrack plane: the DFG phenylalanine ring measured against the αC-Glu+4 Cα and against the catalytic lysine Cα.

The dashed line is the published 11.0 Å threshold. It is drawn because of where it falls: the two classes separate by 8.5 Å on the difference between the two distances, and the line passes through the corner of the DFG-out cluster, which is why six of ABL1's own DFG-out references were being called neither state. Reading the state from which anchor the ring is nearer needs no threshold where the data is dense, and takes agreement with KLIFS from 11 of 17 to 17 of 17.

Why the core matters

Every motif distance here is measured after superposing on this core, so the core is the frame of reference the whole score is expressed in. It is chosen to be the part of the fold that does not move on activation; if it did move, the motif distances would report the core's motion instead.
